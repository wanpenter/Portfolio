Overview

iPETRO — Zero Leak Challenge is an industrial Virtual Reality training simulation built for a Flange Management System (FMS) — the equipment and procedure used to safely assemble and seal pipe flanges in oil & gas facilities.

Rather than training on live equipment, trainees put on a headset and practice the full flange assembly and bolt-tightening sequence to the ASME PCC-1 standard, repeating it until the procedure is correct — with zero real-world risk. The project won a Gold Award at the FTMK Workshop II Innovation Competition.

02

My role

I worked as the VR Developer on this project, building the interaction system in Unity with the XR Interaction Toolkit, designing the diegetic UI, and scripting the bolt-tightening sequence logic that enforces the correct procedure.

03

Technical breakdown

Diegetic UI system

In-world UI panels — not a floating HUD — guide trainees through each maintenance step, keeping the simulation immersive and reducing disorientation common in VR training tools.

Bolt-tightening sequence logic

A step-sequenced interaction system enforces the correct ASME PCC-1 star-pattern tightening order, validated through XR Interaction Toolkit grab-and-rotate interactions.

Interactive maintenance workflow

The full flange assembly workflow — inspect, align, bolt, torque-check — is scripted as a state machine that only advances once each step is completed correctly.

Industrial safety training framing

The simulation is designed to let trainees fail safely, unlike a real flange line, and repeat the procedure until they can complete it correctly and confidently.

04

Challenges

01

Making a physical procedure feel right in VR

Translating a real bolt-tightening star pattern into VR controller interactions, without the physical wrench-feel of the real tool, took several iterations to make the grab-and-rotate interaction read as correct without being fiddly.

02

Guiding without a floating HUD

Committing to a diegetic-only UI meant every instruction had to live inside the 3D scene itself — in-world panels and highlights — rather than reaching for an easy floating text overlay.

03

Designing for safety-critical accuracy

Because the sequence trains a real industrial standard, it had to strictly enforce the correct ASME PCC-1 order and clearly communicate mistakes back to the trainee rather than silently letting an incorrect step pass.
